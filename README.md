# Hi, I'm Michael

I'm an experimental researcher interested in how the brain and body construct perception, especially in cases where sensation, prediction, and self-representation interact.

My broader research interests include:

- Multisensory integration
- Body boundary perception
- Embodiment
- Psychophysics
- Prediction error
- Neural correlates of consciousness
- Atypical sensory processing disorders, including autism and schizophrenia

## Research Background

I first started using GitHub during my [undergraduate senior thesis research](https://github.com/mmccoy-01/RTI). At the time, I did not fully understand GitHub, but I knew it represented a programming and research best practice. I stuck with it, and over time, it became an important part of how I think about reproducibility, transparency, and long-term organization in science.

During my undergraduate work, I became interested in experimental approaches to perception, embodiment, and multisensory processing. That experience helped shape my interest in how subjective experience can be studied through careful behavioral design.

After undergrad, I continued developing as a researcher at the Children’s Hospital of Philadelphia, where I gained experience in a rigorous biomedical research environment. That work helped me appreciate the importance of clear experimental design, careful documentation, and data practices that make research understandable not only in the present, but also for future researchers.

I am now a PhD student at Rowan University, where my current work focuses on developmental and sensory biology. My research examines Hedgehog signaling, the Gas1 co-receptor, and postnatal taste organ morphogenesis, with a broader interest in how biological systems build, maintain, and adapt sensory structures over time.

## Research Philosophy

I try to incorporate strong data practices into my research for the clarity of posterity. Science, to me, is an iterative process: we approach understanding asymptotically, moving closer to the truth while recognizing that we may never fully grasp it.

In that sense, research feels a bit like Tantalus reaching for the fruit — always striving toward something just beyond reach. But the striving itself matters. Each experiment, analysis, figure, and repository is part of that process; it's a feature not a bug.
